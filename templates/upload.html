What FinSight can read

A statement downloaded from your bank, as CSV or Excel, works as it is: FinSight finds the row of column names below the account details. If it can’t tell which column is which, it asks once and remembers your answer for statements with the same columns. Any other file needs these columns:

ColumnExampleNotes
Date2025-01-31YYYY-MM-DD is safest. Day first, such as 31/01/2025, works too; if a date could be read two ways, FinSight asks.
Category (optional)GroceriesAny name you use. Leave it out or blank and FinSight suggests one for you to check.
Amount2450.50A positive number, without ₹ or commas.
TypeExpenseIncome or Expense. Dr and Cr work too.
DescriptionWeekly groceriesAnything that helps you recognise it.

How categories are chosen

Bank statements usually have a description such as “UPI/ZOMATO/4521…” but no category. For rows without one, FinSight suggests a category per merchant and shows you every suggestion before anything is saved.

  1. Your corrections come first. When you change a merchant’s category, it is remembered for your account and always wins next time.
  2. Then keyword rules. After reference numbers, dates and UPI handles are removed, a fixed list of words is checked, such as ZOMATO for Dining or BESCOM for Utilities.
  3. Then a small model that compares letter patterns with about 400 made-up example descriptions. It never learns from your data. If it is less than 60% sure, the merchant is marked “Needs a category” for you to choose.
